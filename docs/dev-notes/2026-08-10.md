**The prediction held on both things it was a prediction about.** The second

what happened, and the claim it drew from them held too: the first run reported

**Where it was wrong, in the order the errors matter.**

1. **The size, and the miss is instructive rather than a rounding error.**
   Predicted "near 318 MB", actual 322.7. The forecast took 267.9 MB and added
   one `business_locations` snapshot at 49.7, and stopped. It left out the day's
   delta growth, which is the same 4.86 MB/day that the whole of ADR-17's
   threshold argument rests on: the 08-10 ingest also wrote a `311_cases` and a
   `building_permits` partition. The prediction used the delta rate to prove the
   threshold cannot fire on growth and then omitted it from its own sum. The
   error is small and the class of it is not, because a floor built by ignoring
   the term that never stops growing is the one that drifts.

2. **The clock, and nothing in the design depended on the part that held.** Both
   crons ran late. `ingest.yml` is `17 9 * * *` and started 10:38:06Z;
   `retention.yml` is `29 11 * * 1` and started 12:08:56Z, about 40 minutes
   behind. The ordering the prediction rests on, ingest first so the check weighs
   the day's growth, held. It held because both slipped, not because they are
   two hours apart. `dbt.yml` is `23 10 * * 1` and started 11:28:47Z, one minute
   ahead of retention's nominal slot: the odd-minute spacing in `retention.yml`'s
   comment is spacing against a schedule GitHub does not honour to the minute.
   Nothing collided today and the reason is luck, not the gap.

3. **Object count went unpredicted and is the cheap check.** 191, from 174 plus
   the 17 the 08-10 ingest wrote: 8 Parquet and 1 manifest for
   `business_locations`, 1 and 1 each for the two delta sets, and 4 manifests for
   the reference sets that fetched nothing and wrote no Parquet. That last four
   is ADR-14's "manifests for runs that wrote no rows are never touched" visible
   in a count.

**ADR-14's acceptance test is not what was run here, and reporting these six as
its result would be false.** The test is prune, rebuild, no count moves, and it
carries its argument only when nothing else changed across the window. The 08-10
ingest landed at 10:38Z, before the apply and after the baseline, so this
comparison spans a prune *and* a day of new data. Six moved counts is what that
window predicts. The clean test was available for about ninety minutes this
morning and nobody was in it.

**What can be shown instead, from surviving data and without taking the tool's
word for anything.** The prune touched exactly one dataset, so that is the only
one needing an argument:

- The 08-09 partition alone holds **365,115 distinct `uniqueid`**, which is the
  baseline `stg_datasf__business_locations` count exactly. Every key the staging
  model returned at baseline was already in a partition the prune kept, so the
  three deleted partitions contributed no distinct key to that number.
- 08-10 is a strict superset of 08-09: **0 keys in 08-09 and not in 08-10, 8 the
  other way**. So the post-prune staging count is 365,115 + 8 and the 8 are new
  businesses, not a coincidence of losses and gains netting out.
- The rest reconciles arithmetically and exactly. `stg_spatial__point_geography`
  is the sum of the four point staging models on both sides:
  123,544 + 38,138 + 365,115 + 2,214 = 529,011, and
  128,062 + 38,138 + 365,123 + 2,214 = 533,537. `int_point_activity` is that
  minus `film_locations`, which it excludes deliberately, minus the undated rows
  its final `where event_month is not null` drops: 706 at baseline, 704 now.
  Nothing is unaccounted for in either column.

**So no count moved because of the prune, and all six moves are the 08-10
ingest.** That is a weaker sentence than the acceptance test would have produced,
and it is the one the evidence supports. It rests on measurements of partitions

## Findings

**The workflow's post-apply projection is now a measured quantity and it was
exact.** "After a full apply of this plan, 173.8 MB" against a measured
173,751,272 bytes. That line was written as an aid to a human reading a report,
and it turns out to be a testable claim that passed on its first outing. It is
the thing that makes the report actionable rather than merely informative, since
it answers "is it worth my running the apply" without the reader doing
arithmetic.

**ADR-17's 170 MB floor is real and the runway numbers should be restated from
it.** The floor was an estimate; the zone is now sitting on it at 173.8 MB. At
the delta rate of 4.86 MB/day the threshold is **170 days** away and the 5 GB
allowance is **993 days** away, against ADR-14's estimate of about 977 with the
prune running. The floor is not static and the note should not imply it: it is
two `business_locations` partitions plus the reference sets plus every delta
partition ever written, and only the last term grows.

**The local zone still holds both datasets ADR-16 cut, and `check_runs` still
warns there.** `make check-runs` with no `.env` sourced: 19 manifests, 0 failing,
and two warnings naming `raw_city_budget` and `raw_street_trees`. ADR-16's
consequence, "runs clean with no warnings for the first time since 2026-08-04",
is true of the bucket and only of the bucket. That is not a defect and it is
worth writing down once, because the warning is the load-bearing signal ADR-16
says must never be softened, and a reader who sees it fire locally after reading
that ADR will reasonably think the scope deletion did not take. `data/raw` is a
different zone and costs nothing, so nothing was deleted here.

**The warning already cites the ADR.** Its text is now "raw_city_budget is in the
zone and not in the registry, so nothing reads it. Reconciled anyway. Delete the
prefix by hand if the cut is final; see ADR-16." That is ADR-16's revisit clause
implemented rather than just asserted, and it was in place before this session.

**`dbt.yml`'s weekly BigQuery build ran against the pre-prune zone and is not
stale in the way that sounds.** Run `31383681415`, success in 2m34s at 11:28:47Z,
which is 45 minutes before the retention report and hours before the apply. Its
external tables were views over a zone holding five `business_locations`
partitions. The marts it built are unaffected, because staging deduplicates to
distinct `grain_key` and the 08-10 partition was already present and is a
superset of every partition since deleted. What did change under BigQuery is the
raw external tables' row counts, since those are the zone's rows and not
deduplicated ones. Nothing was run against BigQuery this session.

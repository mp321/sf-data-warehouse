## What the spec being written first was worth

It decided things this session would otherwise have decided badly, and the two
that mattered most were the two the prompt singled out as easy to implement as
warnings. Both are hard failures:

- **An entry in `prose.yml` that claims a target must resolve every citation
  against that target.** One rule doing two jobs, as section 7 says: it keeps
  the prose honest about the warehouse and `applies_to` honest about the target.
  It has already earned its keep in a small way, since writing the refusals
  meant checking every column name against the warehouse rather than against
  memory, and three of them were wrong on the first pass.
- **Refusals are never dropped to fit the budget.** The ladder drops surplus
  examples, then the undocumented-column markers, then profile statistics, then
  low-signal columns, and then it fails. Measured on the real pack: everything
  present is about 25,300 estimated tokens, and the ladder bottoms out at
  16,400, so the drops are worth 35 percent and none of them touches a refusal.

## The spec needed interpreting in three places, and one of them is a question

Recorded in the plan step in full, and in the module headers where the code is.
The first two are readings; the third is worth a decision from a human.

1. Section 9's second drop stage, "column descriptions for columns with no yml
   description", has nothing to drop as written. The renderer emits an explicit
   `(no description in the yml)` marker, which is a real signal under the
   closed-world rule, and stage 2 drops that.
2. Section 4.4's joins and section 7's "nothing derivable in the prose" are in
   tension, because dbt's `relationships` tests already declare seven joins.
   Those seven are derived from the manifest and `prose.yml` carries the six no
   test declares. A prose join restating a derived one fails the build.
3. **Section 9's rendering order does not include the traps block**, so the
   markdown does not carry it and the JSON does. That is the direction section 3
   allows, and it may still be an oversight in the spec rather than a decision.
   Left as an open question on the plan rather than settled here, because
   amending the contract in the session that first implements it is the wrong
   way round.

## Two things worth knowing before touching this again

**Generate the pack after `make build`, not after `make check`.** The pack
records the dbt invocation in `dbt/target/manifest.json` as the build its
numbers came from, and `make ci-build` overwrites that manifest with the fixture
build's while leaving `data/sf.duckdb` alone. Generating in between produces a
pack whose row counts are the real warehouse's and whose invocation id is a
fixture run's. Nothing detects it: `context-pack-check` deliberately does not
compare invocation ids, because that would fail after every dbt run. The
Makefile comment says so.

**The newest complete month in a date profile comes from the data, not the
clock.** It is the month before the month the column's maximum falls in.
Reading it off today's date would move the pack at midnight on the first of the
month with no data having moved, which would make step 4's drift diff a monthly
false alarm.

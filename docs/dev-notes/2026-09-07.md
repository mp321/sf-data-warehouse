## Findings

**The proof's two limbs are the diagnosis and the report has always printed
both.** `prune_raw.py` reports "N of M grain_key(s) absent, K present at an
older `_socrata_updated_at`". Absent is limb 1 and stale is limb 2. On all 19
unproven partitions K is zero. ADR-18's revisit clause predicts a limb 2
failure, `refresh` having become a lie, and what arrived is a limb 1 failure,
which means something else entirely: the city withdraws records, so a later
partition is complete and is not a superset. The information to tell those
apart was in the log on 2026-08-24 and the clause pointed at the wrong fix.

**The refusal is permanent and the arithmetic says how permanent.** Only
partitions written between the last withdrawal and the keep window are ever
provable. On 2026-09-07 that was three: 09-03, 09-04, 09-05. Withdrawal cohorts
in the report were 6 keys for 08-14 to 08-20, 8 for 08-21, 22 for 08-22, 19 for
08-23 to 08-25 and 18 for 08-26 to 09-02, so a withdrawal event roughly weekly
resets the provable window to a few days.

**27 withdrawn records are in the marts right now.** `stg_datasf__business_locations`
returns 366,491 keys; `ingest_date=2026-09-07` holds 366,464. The difference is
records the city's registry no longer carries, surviving because staging unions
every partition and takes the newest version per key. This is the sentence that
makes relaxing the proof data loss rather than a shortcut: those rows are
reachable today, so deleting the partitions holding them would move mart row
counts, which ADR-18 section 8 defines as the signal to stop pruning.

**Two runs of `business_locations` landed on 2026-08-15**: 730,755 rows,
365,400 distinct keys. This decided the guard's design. A row-count guard would
have read 365,307, then 730,755, then 365,410 and called the third partition a
50% collapse. Distinct `grain_key` is the only count that is stable across
however many times a day the pipeline ran.

**`ingest.py` never read `refresh`.** The registry field that decides whether
the zone may delete anything had no effect on what was fetched. Snapshot
partitions were complete because DataSF bumps `:updated_at` across the whole
registry on a bulk refresh, which made a watermark fetch return everything.
Nothing in the code required that, and three things downstream now assume it.

**The `make load` step stalled once against GCS and it was not a bandwidth
limit.** The first attempt sat for 26 minutes at 0 bytes/s with the process at
0% CPU and one ESTABLISHED connection; `nettop` showed 9.2 MB of out-of-order
receives and 1,328 retransmits on it. Killed and re-run, the same load finished
in **79 seconds**. Worth knowing before concluding that a remote rebuild is
inherently slow: it is not, and a hung `gcsfs` read looks exactly like one.

## Not done

- **Step 4.** The shared macro restricting snapshot staging models to the
  `grain_key`s in the newest `ingest_date`, applied to every snapshot model.
  Not started. It is the step that moves mart row counts once, and the ADR says
  the movement must be captured per model as evidence rather than read as ADR-18
  section 8's failure, which is about a prune moving counts.
- **Any change to the superset proof.** Out of scope by instruction and refused
  in ADR-19 option A on its own merits.
- **`check-snapshots` in `BUILD_PREREQS`.** Deliberate, argued in the module
  docstring, the Makefile comment and the ADR: today a short partition makes the
  zone wrong and the build correct, so gating on it would wedge the pipeline on
  a condition with no consequence. It has to go in with step 4.
- **`check-snapshots` in `retention.yml`.** Not asked for and not obviously
  right: the retention job answers a bytes question and this answers a
  completeness one.
- **The context pack was not regenerated.** No model's schema changed, so its
  per-model hashes are unaffected; the row counts it carries were already
  understood to be stale by design.

   newest partition, is an older one unreachable enough to tell `prune_raw.py`
   so? It needs a new acceptance test, because "no model's row count moves" is
   satisfied trivially by a staging model that already ignores the partition
   being deleted. Until it is taken, the zone's floor is 1299.9 MB and rising.

   until 2 lands. That is now correct behaviour rather than a defect, and
